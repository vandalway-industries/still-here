# R6 — What a 1997 small-business web page actually looked like

> Research item R6. Q12 commissions Vandalway's own page: "a one-page site from the 1990s, never
> updated," and Diane's open list names "hit counter, under-construction banner, 'best viewed in'
> badge." The brief asks that it read "as found, not as parody." Rather than rely on anyone's
> memory, I went and looked at the pages themselves. Memory, including mine, is what produces
> parody. (Petra, 2026-10-03)

## Method

1. **Where small businesses were listed.** Yahoo!'s directory, archived by the Wayback Machine:
   *Companies › Food › Condiments › Jams and Jellies* (1996-10-19) [1], *Companies › Gifts › Gift
   Baskets* (1997-01-11) [2], *Companies › Food › Condiments › Honey* (1997-01-10) [3]. The jam
   category was chosen deliberately, to match the period of Vandalway's own page.
2. **Their pages, as they were.** For each listed business I took the earliest Wayback capture
   dated 1996–1998 and read the **raw archived HTML** (the `id_` form, which omits the Archive's
   own toolbar). The Archive began refusing my requests partway through, so the sample is **13
   business pages** (captures dated 1996-12-21 to 1998-12-02; twelve of them 1996–97) rather than
   the 60 I intended. Four other captures were set aside as not being a business's own page: a
   server directory listing, two "we've moved" notices, and a winery profile on a directory site.
3. **Counted** the features below with pattern matching, then **read each page** to correct the
   counts (two browser recommendations were phrased in ways my patterns missed).

A sample of thirteen supports "common" and "absent" only within this sample. I state the counts and
do not extrapolate from them.

### Five pages, read closely (the jam category, 1997)

- **Fontana Farms** (1997-02-14) [4]: tiled background GIF over a tan `BGCOLOR`, dark-red text,
  centred logo JPEG, four long paragraphs of family-farm prose ("Imagine, if you will, thick fruit
  preserves…"), phone hours, a mailto, `<HR>`, three text links, then the hosting company's image
  map, menu, and copyright 1995-1996. A server-side hit counter sits in an HTML comment at the top.
  The page lives at a path on a web-marketing firm's domain.
- **Joy's** (1997-07-04) [5]: FrontPage 2.0. "*Since 1981*", "*Enhanced for Netscape®*", a
  catalogue "for 1995/1996" with "All prices good until **July, 1996**", which was a year out of date
  when it was captured. That is a never-updated page in the wild. It closes "*Thanks for visiting!
  You come back again!*" and a counter "since October 15, 1996."
- **McCutcheon's** (1997-04-12) [6]: white background, centred logo GIF with `WIDTH=410 HEIGHT=191`,
  a row of seven picture buttons repeated as "[ Nutrition Facts ] [ Our History ] [ Recipes ] …",
  the 1-800 number, a "Page Design By … Version 1.11" credit in tiny lime-green type, links to the
  designer's recumbent-bicycle page, and a counter "since June 6 th 1996."
- **Mountain Maid** (1997-07-02) [7]: an image map, one bold paragraph, a bulleted "More about…"
  link, a bracketed menu for the regional food mall that hosts it, "Copyright © 1995."
- **Sunfresh Foods** (1997-07-15) [8]: a two-cell table (strawberry GIF beside an `<H1>`), three
  links, a centred block of address, telephone, fax and e-mail, a counter "people since April 7,
  1996," "Created for Sunfresh Foods by … Computer Consulting," copyright "1996, 1995."

### Where they lived

In the 1996 jam category, **13 of 16** businesses were a **path on someone else's server** (a
designer's, a provider's, or an online "mall"); two had their own domain (R1 has the full count).
By late 1998 two gift-basket pages in my sample had become notices: "WE'VE MOVED TO A NEW SITE!"
(from a provider's member pages to a `.com`) and "Our gift boxes have been relocated to: …".

## What the secondary sources add

- **Under construction signs** belonged to the amateur web. Olia Lialina's study of the vernacular
  web: "Neither the 'Under Construction' sign nor the idea of permanent construction made it into
  the professional web," and around 1997 the sign became "a meaningless footer and became a common
  joke," replaced by "Last Updated" notices [9]. That matches the sample: zero signs, two
  "Last Updated" lines.
- **"Best viewed with…" buttons** were "first popularized … by Netscape and Microsoft during the
  browser wars of the late 1990s" [10]. They were common enough that a campaign opposed them,
  quoting a July 1996 remark that anyone who adds such a label "appears to be yearning for the bad
  old days" [11]. In the sample they appear only as text.
- **Hit counters** "were popular in the 1990s" and "were often accompanied by the date it was set up
  or last reset," often in an odometer style [12]. Confirmed in every counter above.
- **The markup** is HTML 3.2, a W3C Recommendation of 14 January 1997: `FONT` (`SIZE`, `COLOR`),
  `CENTER`, `BASEFONT`, and `BODY` `BGCOLOR` / `BACKGROUND` / `TEXT` / `LINK` / `VLINK` / `ALINK`.
  `BLINK`, `MARQUEE` and frames are not in it [13]. `BLINK` was Netscape's and never supported by
  Internet Explorer; `MARQUEE` was Internet Explorer's and not Netscape's [14].
- **Colour:** the 216-colour "web-safe" palette existed because "many displays were only capable of
  displaying 256 colors"; its channel values are `00 33 66 99 CC FF` [15]. (Tan `#CEA56B` and
  lime `#80FF00` in the sample are *not* web-safe. People used them anyway.)
- **Type:** browser default was a Times-style serif. Microsoft's Core Fonts for the Web (Arial,
  Verdana, Georgia, Comic Sans MS, Times New Roman and others) ran from 1996 to 2002 [16].

## How it renders today (tested 2026-10-03)

An HTML 3.2 page with its period `<!DOCTYPE HTML PUBLIC "-//W3C//DTD HTML 3.2 Final//EN">` renders
in **quirks mode** (`document.compatMode` = `BackCompat`) in both Chromium 153 and Playwright's
WebKit 26.6. `BGCOLOR`, `TEXT`, `LINK`, `CENTER`, and `FONT SIZE=5 COLOR=…` all take effect.
`<MARQUEE>` is still a working element. `<BLINK>` is an `HTMLUnknownElement` and **does not
blink**. The current HTML standard lists all of these as obsolete for authors while still defining
them for browsers [17]. A period page therefore works as written, with no imitation CSS required,
except that blinking text cannot be had without faking it.

## Recommendation

**Model the page on the sample, not on what people remember of the era.** Concretely, in the
proportions above: one long page; period doctype (quirks mode); upper-case tags; a tiled background
GIF over a `BGCOLOR`; a centred logo GIF with `WIDTH`/`HEIGHT`; a "Welcome to…" paragraph of
earnest company prose; `<HR>` between sections; a bracketed text menu; telephone hours with a time
zone; a `mailto:`; a hit counter worded "This page has been visited [n] times since <date>"; a
dated "Last Updated" line; a credit to whoever built it; a copyright year range. The Sunday-market
photo should be a small JPEG, in the 200–410-pixel range the sample's images used.

On Diane's three named assets:

- **Hit counter: yes.** It is the most period-specific element in the sample (visible on 5 of 13). Given "no
  analytics script anywhere" (`## Organized`), it must not count anything. Two honest forms exist,
  and the author chooses: a fixed image of digits frozen on the day the page stopped being updated,
  which matches "never updated," or a broken image, because 4 of the 5 visible counters were served by
  other companies' CGI services. Whether those services still exist I did not check; a broken image
  assumes they do not.
- **"Best viewed in" badge and under-construction banner: authentic, but not for every page.**
  Neither appears in this sample of business pages. The literature places the construction sign on
  amateur pages and calls it a joke by 1997. They fit if **the page was built in-house**: a
  home-made page plausibly carries both, and "*Enhanced for Netscape*" or "*best viewed with
  Netscape*" as **text** is the form the sample shows. A page built by a hired web firm would carry
  the firm's credit line instead. Who built it is the author's decision, and it decides these two.
- **Avoid stacking** `BLINK` + `MARQUEE` + construction GIF + several animated GIFs + MIDI. None of
  those appeared in the sample, and the combination is the parody the brief rules out.
