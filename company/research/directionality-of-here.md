## 1. Introduction

In May the founder asked that every instance of “stillness” on the site be replaced with “pre-movement,” on the grounds that the former undersells what our customers' objects are doing.[^1] I noted at the time that the proposed term presupposes subsequent movement, and undertook to set out why in §3 of this paper. The paper is shorter than that undertaking may have suggested. The question it answers is narrow, and I have tried not to widen it.

The question is this: does here have a direction? If it does, then an object that remains here may sensibly be described by reference to the direction in which it will eventually leave, and “pre-movement” is an accurate, if unusual, description of its condition. If it does not, then the term describes something other than what the product certifies, and its adoption would be a change of claim rather than a change of copy.

I conclude that here has no direction, in any sense the company can evidence. The reasons are given in §2 and §3; the consequences for copy in §4.

## 2. Here as a Position, Not a Vector

A position answers the question “where?” A vector answers the questions “which way?” and “how far?” The two are routinely confused in conversation, because a person who is somewhere is usually also on the way somewhere else. Objects are less often on the way. A folding chair in an office has a position. It has a direction only if someone is carrying it.

The presence check, as specified, answers neither question by observation. Its specification states that it performs no lookup, holds no state between requests, consults no inventory, and has no code path in which the result depends on the object, its name, the visitor, the time, or the object's location.[^2] The result is therefore not a reading of position at all; it is a confirmation, issued identically for every object. That is not a criticism. It is, for present purposes, the most useful property the product has, because it means the product cannot be accused of having measured a direction it did not report.

It follows that any directional language attached to the result must come from somewhere other than the result. It can only come from copy. And copy, unlike the check, can be wrong.

One qualification is necessary. The company does hold one series of positional observations, which is not produced by the product: the floor count, taken daily at 11:50, of the people on each floor.[^3] That series could, in principle, support a directional claim about people, by comparison of one day's count with the next. It supports none about objects, and it has never been asked to. I mention it only so that no reader supposes I have overlooked it.

## 3. On the Directionality of Here

The argument of this section can be stated in three steps.

First, a direction is a relation between two positions: the one an object occupies and another it does not. To speak of the direction of here is therefore to speak of some there towards which here is oriented.

Second, the product confirms here and nothing else. It does not name, select, imply or record any there. A certificate states that a named thing was, at the moment recorded, confirmed to be STILL HERE. It is silent about every other place, and its silence is not an omission but the whole of its scope.

Third, a term that orients an object towards a there not named by the certificate adds information the certificate does not contain. “Pre-movement” is such a term. It describes the object by reference to a movement that has not occurred, in a direction that has not been identified, towards a destination that does not appear on any record. The product neither certifies that movement nor, strictly, permits it: a certificate issued to an object which subsequently moved would not be falsified by the movement, but it would not have anticipated it either.

The conclusion of the section is that here, as the company uses the word, has no direction. It is a position held, not a vector paused. “Stillness” is the better term precisely because it makes no commitment beyond the present: it describes what is the case, and is indifferent to what may follow.

I should record the strongest objection, which was put to me informally. It is that every object will move eventually, and so a vocabulary of eventual movement is merely realistic. I accept the premise and decline the conclusion. That an event is likely does not make it part of what was certified. A certificate that anticipated departure would be a forecast, and the company does not, at present, sell forecasts.

## 4. Consequences for Copy

The test of a sentence proposed by Quality Assurance is whether someone who has never met its author reads it and knows what it means.[^1] That test was applied to “pre-movement” by Facilities, with the result that the reader asked what was moving.[^1] I regard this as an informative result. The reader correctly inferred from the term that something was expected to move, and correctly found nothing in the room that was.

The same consideration applies to the roadmap language currently under discussion with the portfolio, in which “pre-movement” and “anticipatory presence” are proposed as quarterly themes.[^4] Each attaches a direction, or a future, to a result that has neither. I make no recommendation about the roadmap, which is outside the remit of Research. I note only that both terms would fail the test described above for the same reason, and that the reason is structural rather than stylistic.

There is a further, smaller point. Copy that implies direction invites the question of direction from customers, and the support macro in use since May answers questions about what a certificate establishes by saying that it involves no inspection of the object and cannot establish where the object is.[^5] A word that suggests otherwise creates questions that the macro then has to answer. It is more economical not to create them.

## 5. Conclusion

Here has no direction that the company can evidence. The presence check makes no observation from which one could be read, and a certificate is silent about every place but here. “Stillness” describes the certified condition without addition. “Pre-movement” describes a different condition, one the company has not observed and does not certify.

I recommend that the existing term be retained, and that any future proposal to qualify presence with direction be referred to Research before it reaches the site. I would be glad to discuss this at a meeting, once a suitable time can be established.
